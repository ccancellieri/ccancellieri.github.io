Problem: connect environmental measurements to monitoring context

Environmental monitoring needs measurements to be interpreted in the context of the places and activities they describe. The project supported civilian construction monitoring, with engineering scope coordinated across consortium partners.

My contribution included integration of two datasets through a shared field and presenting environmental measurements through graphs. The case describes this verified scope rather than inferring sensor networks or a deployment stack.

Architecture: linked datasets and monitoring views

The conceptual overview connects two source datasets through their common field, then exposes environmental measurements in graph views. It shows responsibilities supported by my project account, not an inventory of production services.

Joining datasets is a domain decision as well as a data operation. The shared field needs a stable meaning for the resulting monitoring view to remain interpretable.

Context

Decisions: connect technical work to an agreed scope

As R&D lead, I coordinated engineering delivery, Agile practices and scope with consortium partners. That connects the data-integration work to the practical question of which monitoring views the team needed to deliver.

A retrospective review would compare a validated common-field join with manual matching between reports. Manual work can handle exceptions but is difficult to reproduce consistently. A join still requires explicit checks for missing, duplicated or changing identifiers; those are review criteria, not claimed historical failures.

Outcomes: an environmental monitoring contribution

My professional record establishes the 2018–2019 SIGEO role and TAP/SMAI scope. My contribution combined environmental data integration, graph presentation and engineering delivery coordination.

This case does not claim a quantified environmental impact, regulatory approval, sensor throughput, reduced incident rate or sole authorship of the wider monitoring system.

Learning: a shared identifier needs a shared meaning

A graph can appear convincing even when a join is wrong. A technical review should follow the shared field from each dataset into the resulting view and explain its domain meaning.

The delivery lesson is equally concrete: technical scope, stakeholder expectations and reviewable data views need to be coordinated together. For a new version I would validate unmatched and duplicate keys and graph lineage before expanding the interface.
